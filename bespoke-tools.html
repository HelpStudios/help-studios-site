Bespoke models

From each person’s own tracker to one master and its MI.

Employee relations model

Who uses it
Each ER adviser, the ER lead and the HR Director.
What it tracks
Each adviser works in their own tracker and uploads it into one master, which merges every adviser’s grievances, disciplinaries, absence cases and data breaches, so nobody is locked out of a shared spreadsheet.
The MI it gives
The MI produces itself: caseload by adviser and type, days open and time to resolve, ACAS steps recorded, tribunal-risk prompts, FCA conduct flags and manager hot spots.

Recruitment engine

Who uses it
Recruiters, hiring managers and the Head of Recruitment: a team that wants a tracker it can work with rather than a recruitment HRIS.
What it tracks
Job requisitions and openings, every application from applied to hired, sources, recruiters, offers and starts.
The MI it gives
Time to fill and to accept, drop-off by stage, offer acceptance, recruiter and source performance, early leavers by source and the cost of open vacancies, on one dashboard.

Learning dashboard

Who uses it
Each learner, their manager, and the learning and compliance team.
What it tracks
Mandatory modules with due and expiry dates, completions, training delivered and the educators who deliver it.
The MI it gives
Each learner’s own list of what is due, overdue and done; compliance by team and division; what expires next; educator KPIs.

Survey model

Who uses it
HR, People and Culture, and the owners of onboarding and exit.
What it tracks
Questionnaires built for you (new starter, exit, pulse), who is due from the headcount dates, and the send lists.
The MI it gives
Results by theme and statement, a recommend score with its base, a word picture that never keeps comments, and no group under ten shown.

Spreadsheet models made fit

End-user computing, brought under control.

Regulated firms run important figures through spreadsheets built outside formal IT controls. Making them fit for purpose means the same disciplines a system would have.

  1. An inventory

    Every model that matters found and listed, with what it feeds and who relies on it.

  2. Risk tiers

    Each model graded by its impact and complexity, so the critical ones get the strongest controls.

  3. Owners and documentation

    A named owner, a user guide, the logic written down and a log of every change.

  4. Version history and access

    Who can change it, and every version kept.

  5. Tested

    Checks written to fail if something breaks, and the rebuilt model’s outputs proved against the original before it is switched over.

  6. Rebuilt where it should be

    Where a spreadsheet has outgrown itself, the same logic rebuilt as a model or a report, with nothing lost.
