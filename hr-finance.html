HR and Finance alignment

One set of workforce numbers for HR and Finance.

Executives often receive one pack from HR and one from Finance, each showing the workforce its own way, and the numbers do not agree. The board meeting shifts from planning to questioning where the figures came from. I understand how each function treats the numbers, and I bring them into one agreed, reconciled position.

Owning the truth of your data: stringent data governance, management information, and reconciling HR headcount directly with Finance so the executive board and resource committees never see a conflicting number.

Why the numbers differ

Both sides are usually right. They are counting different things.

  1. Heads or FTE

    HR counts people; Finance budgets full-time equivalents. A part-time employee is one head and half an FTE.

  2. Who counts

    Maternity leave, long-term sickness, secondments, zero-hours and bank staff, agency workers and contractors are counted differently by each side, or not at all.

  3. A day or a month

    Month-end headcount and average headcount over the month answer different questions and rarely match.

  4. Effective dates and pay cut-off

    HR records a change on the day it takes effect; payroll acts at the next cut-off. Starters, leavers and changes land in different months.

  5. Leavers still on payroll

    People who have left are still paid notice or untaken holiday, so payroll carries them after HR has stopped counting them.

  6. Structure and cost centres

    The organisation in the HRIS and the cost centres in the ledger drift apart as teams move and are renamed.

  7. Establishment and budget

    Finance holds approved, budgeted posts; HR holds people in post. Vacancies and over-establishment sit between the two.

  8. Cost, not heads

    The heads can agree while the cost does not: transfers, timing, on-costs and accruals move money without moving people.

What I do

Agree the definitions, reconcile person by person, explain every difference.

Resolving executive-level reporting risk by bringing HR and Finance FTE data into one agreed, reconciled position, and building robust FTE governance models for resource committees.

  1. 01

    Agree the definitions

    One data dictionary HR and Finance both sign: who counts, how FTE is calculated, and which date governs each change.

  2. 02

    Reconcile person by person

    The HRIS, payroll and the ledger joined on the employee ID, so every difference is a named person and a reason, not a balancing figure.

  3. 03

    Explain every variance

    A bridge from one number to the other that always adds up, with the reason for each step.

  4. 04

    A monthly routine

    A reconciliation that runs every month with named owners, so the numbers agree before the pack goes out rather than after the board asks.

  5. 05

    One pack for executives

    One workforce view, with the HR and Finance perspectives side by side and reconciled, instead of two packs that disagree.

  6. 06

    FTE governance

    FTE governance models and establishment control for resource committees: approved posts, vacancies and changes tracked against people in post.
